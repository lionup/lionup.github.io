<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/durables_and_lemons_PAPER.pdf">Durables and Lemons: Private Information and the Market for Cars</a></div>
  <div class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://www.ucl.ac.uk/~uctp39a/">Richard Blundell</a>, <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/sorenleth">Soren Leth-Petersen</a>, <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/hamishlowecon/">Hamish Low</a>, and <a href="https://sites.google.com/yale.edu/costasmeghir/home">Costas Meghir</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue"><em>Quantitative Economics</em>, 2026</div>
  <div class="paper-media">Media: <a href="https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2019/09/26/can-you-buy-a-good-second-hand-car">The Economist</a>, <a href="https://ucleconbrief.co.uk/2026/03/02/the-lemons-penalty-evidence-from-denmarks-market-for-second-hand-cars/">Econ Brief</a>, <a href="https://www.econ.ku.dk/cebi/news/can-you-sell-a-good-used-car-evidence-from-the-danish-car-market/">CEBI News</a>, <a href="https://nepdge.wordpress.com/2019/10/03/durables-and-lemons-private-information-and-the-market-for-cars/#respond">NEP-DGE blog</a></div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/QE1822">paper</a>
    <a class="pill" href="https://zenodo.org/records/17067942">replication</a>
    <a class="pill" href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w26281">NBER WP w26281</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>Private information on car quality means the sale price reflects the average quality of cars sold, which can be lower than the average quality in the population. This difference is the lemons penalty imposed on holders of high quality cars. We estimate the evolution of the lemons penalty through an equilibrium model of car ownership with private information using Danish linked registry data on car ownership, income, and wealth. We examine the aggregate implications and distributional consequences of these penalties. In the first year of ownership, we estimate that the lemons penalty is 12% of the price. The penalty declines sharply with the length of ownership. It reduces the self-insurance value of cars and leads to a large reduction in transaction volumes and the rate of car turnover. The market does not collapse: income shocks induce households to sell their cars, even if they are of good quality, and this helps mitigate the lemons problem. The size of the lemons penalty declines when income uncertainty in the economy increases and when the supply of credit decreases.</div>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/jech-2025-224166.full.pdf">Does the “right to request” flexible work policy influence men’s and women’s uptake of flexible working and well- being - Findings from the UK Household Longitudinal Study</a></div>
  <div class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://profiles.ucl.ac.uk/36824-baowen-xue/">Baowen Xue</a>, <a href="https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/heejung-chung/">Heejung Chung</a>, and <a href="https://profiles.ucl.ac.uk/11074-anne-mcmunn/">Anne McMunn</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue"><em>BMJ's Journal of Epidemiology &amp; Community Health</em>, 2026</div>
  <div class="paper-media">Media: <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/josiecox/2025/09/16/flexible-working-policies-alone-dont-change-workplace-culture-study-shows/">Forbes</a>, <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-02/fathers-face-steeper-wage-cuts-when-stepping-back-from-work?utm_source=website&amp;utm_medium=share&amp;utm_campaign=copy">Bloomberg</a>, <a href="https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/flexible-working-policies-not-enough-to-change-workplace-practices">King's College London News</a></div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="https://jech.bmj.com/content/early/2025/09/13/jech-2025-224166">paper</a>
    <a class="pill" href="/research/jech-2025-224166-inline-supplementary-material-1.pdf">supplement</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>The ‘right to request’ flexible working policy has been gradually extended and, by 2014, extended to cover all workers with at least 26 weeks of continuous employment. The impact of these policy changes is unclear. This research aims to assess the effects of the 2014 policy reform on the uptake of flexible working and its impact on health and well-being, focusing on gender differences. Data were drawn from waves 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 of the UK Household Longitudinal Study (2010–2020). We employed a doubly robust difference-in-differences method to estimate the average treatment effects on the treated of the 2014 policy reform. This analysis examined the effects on the uptake of flexible working, mental and physical health, and satisfaction with life, job and leisure. The 2014 policy reform increased women’s uptake of reduced hours work arrangements, with the effect growing stronger over time. However, no increase in uptake was observed among men. No strong effects were found for flexitime or teleworking arrangements for either men or women. Additionally, the policy reform resulted in a reduction in psychological distress and improved life satisfaction among women. The reduction in women’s psychological distress and improved life satisfaction might be partly explained by the increased women’s uptake of reduced hours arrangements, which may have enabled women to better meet their family care demands. However, even the gender-neutral policies on flexible working may inadvertently exacerbate gender inequalities in labour force participation by pushing women more into part-time work.</div>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/Intra_HH_pub.pdf">The Gender Gap in Household Bargaining Power: A Revealed-Preference Approach</a></div>
  <div class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/cameronpengresearch/">Cameron Peng</a> and <a href="https://www.weilongzhang.com/">Weilong Zhang</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue"><em>Review of Financial Studies</em>, 2024</div>
  <div class="paper-media">Media: <a href="https://theconversation.com/who-really-holds-the-purse-strings-why-it-matters-which-partner-decides-where-the-money-goes-241089">The Conversation</a>, <a href="https://www.essex.ac.uk/news/2024/08/28/husbands-still-dominate-investment-decisions">U of Essex News</a>, <a href="https://phys.org/news/2024-08-husbands-dominate-investment-decisions-reveals.html">Science X</a>, <a href="https://scienmag.com/husbands-still-dominate-investment-decisions">ScienMag</a></div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhae039">paper</a>
    <a class="pill" href="https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/39/6/1611/7738808?login=false">supplement</a>
    <a class="pill" href="https://www.ifs.org.uk/publications/15437">IFS WP21/11</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>When members of the same household have different risk preferences, whose preference matters more for investment decisions and why? We propose an intrahousehold model that aggregates individual preferences at the household level as a result of bargaining. We structurally estimate the model, analyze the determinants of bargaining power, and find a significant gender gap. Gender differences in individual characteristics, as well as gender effects, partially explain the gap. These patterns hold broadly across Australia, Germany, and the United States. We further link the distribution of bargaining power to households’ perceived gender norms in a cross-sectional analysis.</div>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/human-capital-business-cycle.pdf">Human Capital and the Business Cycle Effects on the Postgraduate Wage Premium</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue"><em>Review of Economic Dynamics</em>, 2023</div>
  <div class="paper-media">Media: <a href="http://blogs.lse.ac.uk/businessreview/2018/06/13/a-postgraduate-degree-protects-you-against-the-business-cycle/">LSE Business Review</a>, <a href="http://www.res.org.uk/details/mediabrief/10938521/A-POSTGRADUATE-DEGREE-PROTECTS-YOU-AGAINST-THE-BUSINESS-CYCLE-US-evidence.html">Royal Economic Society</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1920/co.ifs.2024.0093">IFS Comment</a></div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.red.2022.07.001">paper</a>
    <a class="pill" href="https://www.ifs.org.uk/publications/14515">IFS WP19/26</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>Postgraduate degree holders experience lower cyclical variation in real wages than those with undergraduate degrees. Moreover, postgraduate jobs require more specific human capital and take longer to adapt to. Using an equilibrium search model with dynamic incentive contracts, this paper attributes the cyclicality of the postgraduate-undergraduate wage gap to the differences in specific capital. Greater specific capital leads to lower mobility, thereby improving risk-sharing between workers and firms. The estimates of the model reveal that specific capital can explain the differences both in labour turnover and in real wage cyclicality between education groups.</div>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/Gu%20and%20Zhong%202023.pdf">Effects of Stay-at-home Orders on Skill Requirements in Vacancy Postings</a></div>
  <div class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://www.ling-zhong.com/">Ling Zhong</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue"><em>Labour Economics</em>, 2023</div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.labeco.2023.102342">paper</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>The COVID-19 pandemic and containment policies have had profound economic impacts on the labor market. Stay-at-home orders (SAHOs) implemented across most of the United States changed the way of people worked. In this paper, we quantify the effect of SAHO durations on skill demands to study how firms adjust labor demand within occupation. We use skill requirement information from the 2018 to 2021 online job vacancy posting data from Burning Glass Technologies, exploit the spatial variations in the SAHO duration, and use instrumental variables to correct for the endogeneity in the policy duration related to local social and economic factors. We find that policy durations have persistent impacts on the labor demand after restrictions are lifted. Longer SAHOs motivate management style transformation from people-oriented to operation-oriented by requiring more of operational and administrative skills and less of personality and people management skills to carry out standard workflows. SAHOs also change the focus of interpersonal skill demands from specific customer services to general communication such as social and writing skills. SAHOs more thoroughly affect occupations with partial work-from-home capacity. The evidence suggests SAHOs change management structure and communication in firms.</div>
</div>

<div class="paper">
  <div class="paper-title"><a href="/research/epc-pd-6.pdf">Housing Energy Efficiency over the Life Cycle</a></div>
  <div class="paper-authors">With <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/eglejakucionyte/">Egle Jakucionyte</a> and <a href="https://sites.google.com/site/eswapnilsingh/">Swapnil Singh</a></div>
  <div class="paper-venue">October 2026</div>
  <div class="paper-links">
    <a class="pill" href="/research/epc-pd-6.pdf">paper</a>
    <button class="pill pill-abstract" type="button" aria-expanded="false">abstract</button>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-abstract" hidden>We document that older homeowners in England live in less energy-efficient homes. Two channels could explain this. Through the <em>horizon channel</em>, older owners have fewer years to collect the return on a renovation, so they invest less. Through the <em>purchase window</em>, efficiency is bought with the home. As mobility declines with age, older owners have fewer chances to acquire efficiency cheaply. We estimate a lifecycle model of housing, mobility, and renovation on English data. The age gradient comes from the purchase window, not the horizon channel. Efficiency wears off within a few years, so the payoff horizon hardly varies with age, while older owners buy less often. Hence renovation subsidies narrow the age gap, whereas point-of-sale standards widen it.</div>
</div>
