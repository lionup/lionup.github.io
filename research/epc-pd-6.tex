\begin{abstract}
We document that older homeowners in England live in less energy-efficient homes. Two
channels could explain this. Through the \emph{horizon channel}, older owners have
fewer years to collect the return on a renovation, so they invest less.
Through the \emph{purchase window}, efficiency is bought with the home. As mobility declines with age, older owners have fewer chances
to acquire efficiency cheaply. We estimate a lifecycle model of housing, mobility, and renovation on
English data. The age gradient comes from the purchase window, not the
horizon channel. Efficiency
wears off within a few years, so the payoff horizon hardly varies with age,
while older owners buy less often. Hence renovation subsidies narrow the age
gap, whereas point-of-sale standards widen it.

\end{abstract}
